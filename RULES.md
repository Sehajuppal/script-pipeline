## 9. DEEP-DIVE PATCHES — 2026-09-27 (mind/brain × dossier × human-complexity)
Appended by the deep-dive coordinator. Each patch: rule, mechanism, source, evidence grade.
Grades: VERIFIED = replicated/primary; CONTESTED = convergent but unsettled;
INFERENCE = strong theory, thin direct evidence. Lab mechanisms' application to
30s ad scripts is inference — treat lab effect sizes as upper bounds.

### 9A. Brain-level timing rules
- A1. POST-PEAK BLACKOUT GAP: after any high-arousal beat (twist, shock claim,
  loud reveal), no payload info (price, name, link/handle, discount) within 0.8s
  of the peak — insert a low-information buffer (reaction shot, held pause,
  cutaway). Payload lands at peak+0.8s or later. [attentional blink /
  emotion-induced blindness, Raymond/Shapiro/Arnell 1992; Most 2005 — VERIFIED LAB]
- A2. BRAND-AT-PEAK ONLY: brand name, price, handle, discount appear ONLY in the
  hook (0–3s) and at/after the payoff beat (72–100%). Strip them from low-arousal
  filler beats — arousal starves low-priority items from memory. Re-anchor the
  brand once in the comment-bait beat (85–94%). [arousal-biased competition,
  Mather & Sutherland 2011; Shields 2017 k=113 — VERIFIED]
- A3. END-VALENCE RULE: final 2s CTA must be gain-framed and positive
  ("Save this", "your mornings, upgraded"). No loss, guilt, or pressure framing
  in the close — the end term dominates brand-attitude memory. [peak-end rule,
  Kahneman/Fredrickson 1993 — VERIFIED]
- A4. TRANSIENT-SYNC BRAND BIND: the product/brand NAME must be SPOKEN at the
  exact moment of a visual transient (on-screen text pop or cut) at least once
  at the payoff beat; that on-screen name is the beat's single visual isolate
  (sole color/scale violation). [AV pip-and-pop, van der Burg 2008; von Restorff
  — VERIFIED LAB]
- A5. CHUNK BUDGET: each beat carries ≤3 new propositional chunks
  (one claim + one proof + one label, max). Overflow doesn't queue — it drops.
  [working memory, Cowan 2001 — VERIFIED]
- A6. AROUSAL TRAJECTORY, NOT EMOTION LABELS: script every video as an arousal
  curve — baseline curiosity (hook) → dip (unhurried trust beat) → rise (demo) →
  peak (payoff) → positive resolve (CTA). Never brief "make them feel joy/anger";
  emotions have no neural fingerprints. [dimensional valence×arousal, Lindquist
  2012 k=91; Siegel 2018 k=202 — VERIFIED]
- A7. PAYOFF MUST VIOLATE, NOT MERELY FULFILL: the payoff beat contains one
  explicit expectancy mismatch (a prediction the viewer held, shown wrong).
  Contract fulfillment alone leaves reward-prediction-error at zero — and PE is
  what consolidates to 24h memory. [dopamine RPE, Schultz/Dayan/Montague 1997;
  Gruber 2014 (high-curiosity recall 70.6% vs 54.1%); Sinclair 2021 — VERIFIED
  LAB/NARROW; ad application INFERENCE]

### 9B. Dossier-gap structure rules
- B1. STRONG FACTS STAY PLAIN: if the evidence is strong (data, specs, demo
  footage), state it plainly in EDUCATION/DEMO. Wrap only WEAK claims in story.
  Never story-frame a number the viewer can verify. [Krause & Rucker 2020,
  story×fact-quality ηp²=.18 — VERIFIED]
- B2. STORY OPENS, FACTS CLOSE: blended story→fact beat order beats pure
  narrative or pure facts. [JAMS 2025, ηp²=.02 — VERIFIED]
- B3. 0:08 SUBHOOK: at ~0:08 restate the payoff contract once before the first
  swipe decision point — name what the viewer is waiting for. [hooks playbook §12,
  349-reel base — VERIFIED observational]
- B4. LATE BOMB: for 30–60s scripts, place one major interrupt or leak the first
  crumb of the withheld payoff at ~0:25–0:35. [hooks playbook §12 — VERIFIED
  observational]
- B5. BEAT CEILING: cap at ~5 major beats per 30–60s script (>7 correlates with
  lower likes). "Notice the editing" test — if the viewer notices the structure,
  cut one beat. [hooks playbook §13/§15 — VERIFIED observational]
- B6. "WAIT FOR IT" IS BANNED: never use it as a gap device. To hold a curiosity
  gap open, leak exactly one word of the payoff as on-screen text pop.
  [curiosity-gap mechanics — CONTESTED, strong convergence]
- B7. REFUTATIONAL FLAW ADMISSION: in review/pitch formats, admit exactly one
  minor weakness and immediately refute it ("not the cheapest — here's why that's
  fine"). The flaw mention comes from the creator, never left to the comments.
  Boundary: under low elaboration, a one-line caveat AFTER positives also lifts
  evaluation. [Eisend 2006 meta-analysis — VERIFIED credibility effect; Ein-Gar,
  Shiv & Tormala 2012 — CONTESTED]
- B8. PRODUCT GOAL-CENTRAL, CTA AFTER THE HAPPY TURN: the product must CAUSE
  story events (woven into goal pursuit), never appear as a prop. In story
  formats, product reveal/CTA comes after the story's happy turn — ending valence
  drives brand liking. [Strick & Volbeda 2018 — CONTESTED]
- B9. SHOW THE TRANSFORMATION'S MIDDLE: transformation formats script 1–2
  progression beats (process simulation), not just before/after endpoints.
  Exception: under speed framing ("results in 7 days"), skip the middle.
  [Cian, Longoni & Krishna 2020 — VERIFIED]
- B10. NARRATIVE BACKFIRE GUARD: story-shaped sponsored scripts keep sales
  pressure subtle — perceived manipulative intent kills narrative persuasion, and
  disclosure+narrative is the worst combination. Soften the sell or de-risk
  disclosure in story formats. [Li, Ma & Sommerfeldt 2025; Moyer-Gusé & Nabi
  2010 — CONTESTED convergent]
- B11. SPEECH-RATE ASYMMETRY: moderately faster delivery is a credibility cue
  (ceiling ~200 wpm) — but it AMPLIFIES weak pitches and HURTS strong ones.
  Slow down for strong claims, speed up for weak ones. [Miller 1976 →
  Vaughan-Johnston 2024 — VERIFIED]
- B12. ILLUSTRATOR GESTURES; HANDS OFF OBJECTS: presenter gestures what she
  means (size, direction, relation) — causal lift on clarity/competence/
  persuasiveness. Never fidget or play with objects on camera. [Zhou et al. JMR
  2025, 1,600-subject experiments + 200k-gesture observational — VERIFIED]
- B13. GAZE COMFORT WINDOW: eye contact 2–5s at a time; no hard lens-locked
  stare on close-up (threat/reactance). [Fauville et al. 2022 preregistered
  N=3,982; Chen et al. 2013 — VERIFIED]
- B14. FELT SIMILARITY > CENSUS MATCHING: cast/design personas on perceived
  similarity (values, humor, frustrations), not demographics. Same-gender
  advantage exists for female audiences via bonding; presenter gender alone moves
  neither authenticity nor attractiveness. [Tukachinsky 2014 meta d=0.85;
  JTAER 2025 2×2×2 null — VERIFIED + CONTESTED]
- B15. COMPLEXITY IS ITS OWN FIT AXIS: for high-complexity SKUs (finance,
  medical, technical specs), AI presenter is the evidence-favored pick even where
  hedonic-context rules would exclude. Humans keep low-complexity.
  [ai-persona dossier F145 — CONTESTED; extends §4 F32]
- B16. RED BAN IN FINANCE: no red in price graphics/chart overlays for
  money/finance scripts (red suppresses risk appetite in real-money decisions).
  No universal hue chart — calibrate hue to niche associations. [Bazley et al.
  2021 — VERIFIED; Palmer & Schloss 2010 WAVE — VERIFIED]
- B17. LOOP-FRIENDLY ENDINGS (Shorts): for vertical-short variants, end
  mid-sentence or on the payoff question so the loop replays. [hooks playbook
  §14 — CONTESTED observational]
- B18. WARM-AFFILIATIVE DEFAULT: default delivery warm, affiliative,
  agreement-rich. Dominant/authoritative delivery penalizes female presenters
  (esp. with male audiences); aggressive/edgy appeals repel female-skewed
  audiences. [Carli 1989/2001 — VERIFIED + CONTESTED]
- B19. BACKGROUND TWO-JOB RULE: script the setting to do two jobs —
  anchor–background fit (→ trust) AND product–background fit (→ value). The set
  primes the attribute the pitch leans on (~17-pt weighting swing). [Shang et al.
  2023 — VERIFIED; Mandel & Johnson 2002 — CONTESTED]
- B20. MODERATE DISFLUENCY > PERFECT FLUENCY: allow 1–2 natural disfluencies
  (um/uh) in voiceover; moderate disfluency beats perfect fluency for compliance.
  Bound the hyper-fluent edit. [Conrad et al. 2013 — CONTESTED]

### 9C. Human-complexity rules (generatable)
- C1. CONTROLLED-FAILURE BEAT: every presenter=human script includes one
  scripted plan-miss at 0:05–0:15 — attempt one visibly fails (mechanical miss:
  spill, wrong word, missed throw), then a sheepish self-correction and a second
  attempt. The failure must never undermine the product's core claim. The failure
  clip, not the success, is the payoff. [prediction error; recurrence across
  1M+ viral reels — VERIFIED observational]
- C2. ESCALATION LADDER: where a demo repeats attempts, escalate absurdity across
  exactly 3 beats, each visibly bigger than the last; never resolve with a flat
  success — end on a harmless peak. [compounding prediction error — VERIFIED
  observational]
- C3. MYSTERY-DETAIL PLANT: plant exactly one unexplained visual anomaly per
  script (odd background event, mystery prop), never resolved on screen; the
  comment-bait line references it obliquely to seed theory comments.
  [ambiguity gap — VERIFIED pattern; comment-causation INFERENCE]
- C4. WITNESS-REACTOR FRAMING: in every payoff beat, a second human face is
  in-frame reacting to the event (surprise, disbelief, laughter) — reaction aimed
  at the EVENT, never the camera. [vicarious contagion — VERIFIED observational]
- C5. ONE SCRIPTED REPAIR: the education beat contains exactly one mid-sentence
  self-correction ("—wait, no—"). Do not stack with laughs/sighs (§3 F142 still
  applies — this is a repair, not a vocalization); counts toward the B20
  disfluency budget. [disfluency-as-online-thinking — INFERENCE]
- C6. CANDID-GRAMMAR LANE: presenter=human render notes carry ≥2 of: handheld
  micro-shake, uneven natural lighting, mid-sentence zoom punch-in. Polished
  studio grammar is the AI-persona lane; humans get the candid lane. Raw grammar
  is a genre cue for candid framing, not a universal upgrade (§5 already kills
  "raw always wins"). [genre attribution — CONTESTED]
- C7. UNCERTAINTY WINDOW: stage one uncertainty window at ~0:15–0:20 — an
  outcome the presenter reacts to on camera with a visible surprise beat
  (widened framing, half-beat pause, unscripted-sounding line). The script
  decides the outcome; the rendering sells the reaction. [self-generated
  prediction error — INFERENCE]

- "People have 3-second attention spans" (no such construct — capture is
  set-contingent); dopamine=pleasure (DA is wanting/RPE, Berridge & Robinson);
  "more emotion = more memory" (ABC starves peripheral items); Zeigarnik as
  memory law (2025 meta ≈0.99, struck); subliminal brand priming (program dead);
  oxytocin "trust hormone" framing (Nave 2015, Kroll 2026, Winterdahl 2025 PET);
  Yerkes-Dodson as law; left/right-brain appeals; "raw always beats polished";
  "just be spontaneous/authentic" without beat structure; genuine improvisation
  (can't be generated — scripted variance only); real interpersonal chemistry
  (generatable substitute: overlapping-turn dialogue grammar); prank backfire on
  a real victim (requires a genuinely surprised person).
- CONTESTED alternative not encoded: steal-the-thunder admission (name a flaw,
  NO rebuttal) vs B7's refutational rule (admit + refute) — the meta-analytic
  evidence favors refutational; keep steal-thunder as an A/B candidate only.

## 1. HOOK (first 3 seconds decide everything)
- 3-layer stack: VISUAL 0.0–0.5s (mid-action, works muted — 85% watch sound-off)
  → VERBAL 0.5–1.5s (≤8 words, says the payoff) → LOCK by 3.0s
  (viewer can answer: what is this about, and why keep watching?)
- Default hook family: **curiosity-gap** or **bold-claim** (~41% of viral hooks).
  Niche natives: transformation (beauty/food), negative-command (advice/education),
  story-time (lifestyle/vlog), contrarian-reframe (commentary), POV (comedy).
- Payoff contract: name a SPECIFIC reward in the hook, deliver it ~75–80% through.
  Never vague ("you won't believe"), never frontloaded.
- Borrowed authority: connect your point to a name everyone knows
  ("Hormozi's rule for X", not "my strategy").

## 2. STRUCTURE (retention is a ladder, not a hook)
- Re-hook every beat (~7–10s): "but here's the part nobody shows you" —
  open loops pull viewers past the 3–5s mark.
- Educational beats BEFORE evaluative pitch (F103: educational narrative >
  evaluative sales-pitch for virtual personas; null for humans).
- Demo beats: mid-action framing, one test per beat, on-screen TEST labels.
- Payoff lands ~72–85% through. Comment-bait ~85–94%: deliberate ranking or
  fill-in-the-blank ("rank these 3 — fight in the comments").
- CTA LAST (~94–100%), explicit. Never open with a CTA.

## 3. VOICE & DELIVERY
- Pace by objective: unhurried + warm for trust-building beats (F138),
  brisk 160–180 wpm for CTAs (F86). Never pressuring/harsh (reactance).
- One consistent voice per persona from day one. Fluency/quality > humanness.
- No scripted laughs/sighs — volitional vocalizations read inauthentic (F142).
- Whisper-flow register is a second lane for indulgence/self-care SKUs (d=0.38).

## 4. AI-PERSONA CONSTRAINTS (presenter=ai)
- Product fit: AI personas convert on UTILITARIAN SKUs (tech, tools, gadgets).
  Hedonic/experiential SKUs → use a human presenter (F32). High fit + exposed
  deception backfires worse than low fit — guard trust asymmetrically.
- Attractiveness is a top-of-funnel amplifier, NOT a conversion lever.
  Invest in fit + engagement mechanics, not hotter faces.
- Disclosure: quiet and integrated (small on-screen "AI persona" + caption line).
  Never a banner; never omission (third-party exposure = trust cliff, F98).
- Identity: lock the face from video #1 (fixed seed / reference image).
  Drift = defect. Never clone a real known person (migration penalty, F114).
- Rendering: flawless hyperreal OR clean stylized. Detectable synthetic flaws
  (lip-sync, skin artifacts) are the eeriness driver, not mid-realism (F133).
- Bonds: design for perceived AUTONOMY — the persona "decides" things,
  posts unprompted, has opinions (F143: VI-specific PSR driver).
- Engagement ladder: comments → DMs → community → conversion.
  Conversion lags engagement; engagement is the leading indicator (F112).

## 5. HUMAN-UGC NOTES (presenter=human)
- Similarity > beauty for presenter appeal. Warm, affiliative, agreement-rich
  delivery persuades better than dominant task-style (works across genders).
- Selfie distance is fine (viewers are adapted); for high-trust claims
  (price, guarantees) prefer medium shot over extreme close-up.
- Backgrounds work through FIT, not a fixed "raw beats polished" rule.
- Authentic-seeming presenters > unpolished production. Visible craft can help
  via effort cues, but the purchase leg of the effort heuristic failed replication.

## 6. PROOF & SOCIAL PROOF
- Proof-first hooks need VERIFIABLE receipts (viewers audit screenshots).
- Specific numbers beat vague claims ("12,000+ orders", "4.9★").
- Transformation frame: show the AFTER first, then rewind to how it got there.
  The before/after gap must be visible at thumbnail scale.

## 7. CAPTION
- +26% clicks with a caption. Structure: hook line → 2-line summary →
  CTA → niche hashtags. Keep an AI-persona disclosure line for presenter=ai.

## 8. WHAT THE RESEARCH CANNOT TELL YOU (pipeline A/B queue)
- Same-face vs drifting-face conversion test
- Story-format vs non-story on an AI persona
- Hyperreal vs stylized clicks-to-buy (same persona)
- Interactive vs broadcast randomized on a persona
- Any behavioral purchase-DV experiment
Run these yourself; the literature is exhausted here.

### Cycle 1 findings
- D1. COMMENT-WORD CTA ENGINE: for product/demo scripts, end on a SINGLE-WORD
  comment-word CTA ("Comment FRIDGE — I'll send you the full link list"),
  routed to a link list, instead of (or before) "follow for part 2". The word is
  the category or the shoppable object in ALL CAPS, echoed on screen at the
  comment-bait beat and pinned in a reply comment. Comment-word routing converts
  passive intent into a measurable reply the algorithm can see; "follow for part
  2" defers the reward past the session. [julianna_claire 6/6 top ugc-ads reels
  use comment-word CTAs, 1.7–5.4K comments each; engagement-ladder theory —
  VERIFIED observational]
- D2. TEST-ESCALATION TRACKER: demo beats carry a visible progression grammar —
  numbered tests that ESCALATE (each visibly higher-stakes than the last) with an
  on-screen score bug or pass/fail stamp per test; never flat "Test 1 / Test 2"
  of equal weight, and re-hooks are event-specific, never generic
  ("Here's where it gets interesting" is the banned family). The tracker gives
  the viewer a game to finish. Extends C2 (escalation ladder) from the human
  lane to all demo scripts, AI included. [brookemonk on-screen score tracking +
  RPS tie-break resolution; hydraulic-press compilation payoff-every-4s;
  julietta price-anchored restock escalation — VERIFIED observational]
- D3. AI-LANE MYSTERY PLANT: presenter=ai demo scripts plant exactly one
  unexplained visual anomaly (odd background event, strange reflection, mystery
  prop), referenced OBLIQUELY in the comment-bait line ("...and whatever's
  going on in the background, I see you"), never resolved on screen. Extends C3
  to the AI lane — polished AI content needs ambiguity gaps too, since
  commenters theorize about the anomaly regardless of presenter. [ambiguity-gap
  recurrence across niches — VERIFIED pattern; comment-causation INFERENCE]

## 11. ALCHEMY PATCHES — 2026-09-27 (Rory Sutherland, *Alchemy*; via @mrliamrobins reel "How to market like a scientist")

Thesis: psycho-logic beats logic. Never open with the rational pitch (specs, price, features) — open with the perception problem the video solves: how the product FEELS in the viewer's life. Per Rule 8, every product also gets one counterintuitive variant a spreadsheet would reject.

- E1. INVERT THE BRIEF: for every product, generate (a) the logical script, (b) its INVERSION ("why you shouldn't buy this", "the worst reason to own X"), and (c) a trivial-detail variant. Ship all three cheaply; let observation decide. [Sutherland Rule 1: "the opposite of a good idea can also be a good idea" — book]
- E2. REFRAME THE COMPARISON SET: never let the viewer evaluate on the commodity axis. Change the comparison on screen, not the product — "vs the $6 café cup", never "vs the supermarket jar". State the reference price out loud; the product's price is only ever shown against it. [Nespresso reframed price vs the café cup, not the instant jar — book]
- E3. PRICE AS PROOF (placebo pricing): never discount to prove value — discounting can REDUCE perceived efficacy. Script the price as the proof ("the $40 one, because your skin can tell"); show premium cues (dosage specificity, ritual of use) that strengthen the placebo. Testimonials say "I FELT the difference" — the felt difference is the product. [Ariely $2.50 vs 10¢ pill: 85% vs 61% relief — JAMA study via book]
- E4. SIGNAL WITH VISIBLE COST: put expensive-to-fake signals in the video — heavy foil-stamped unboxing, price shown early and confidently, craft shots that look costly to produce. Advertising works partly BECAUSE it's expensive; a high price IS the ad. [Costly signaling — book]
- E5. SELL CERTAINTY, NOT SPECS (uncertainty removal): for anything with delivery, results-over-time, or complexity, script the reassurance INTO the video — order-tracking screen, results timeline ("week 1, week 3, week 6"), progress indicator. Promise certainty ("arrives Thursday, or it's free") over marginally better specs. People pay a premium to remove uncertainty. [Uber's map = "multiplying cabs by ten" without shorter waits — book]
- E6. SATISFICING SCRIPT: the video's job is risk removal, not feature proof. First 10 seconds carry: social-proof counter, reversibility ("30-day no-questions returns"), authority badge. Close with the safe choice as the DEFAULT ("the one everyone switches to"). Buyers avoid disaster; they don't optimize. ["Nobody got fired for buying IBM" logic — book]
- E7. ADMIT THE FLAW FIRST: open with the honest inferiority — "It won't make you rich. It WILL end 6 a.m. scrubbing." The confession earns the next 25 seconds of claims. Especially potent for human-presenter scripts where polish triggers skepticism. [Admission of inferiority builds trust — book]
- E8. NAME THE TRICK: give the method/hack an on-screen name ("The 2-Dishwasher Method" style) so viewers can repeat and share it. Creating a name creates a norm — names become memes, memes become distribution. [Sutherland Rule 5: "a flower is a weed with an advertising budget" — book]
- E9. DARE TO BE TRIVIAL: obsess over micro-details competitors ignore — the first 1.5s hook frame, one added word in the caption, CTA-vs-price reveal ORDER. A publisher added four trivial words to a call-centre script and doubled conversions; Best Buy's $300m button was only a change in ask order. Queue trivial variants for A/B first. [Dare to be trivial — book]
- E10. SELL THE RITUAL, NOT THE RESULT: 10x1 ≠ 1x10. Script the subscription/habit frame explicitly — "9¢ a day" beats "$29 a month"; "30 seconds every morning" beats "15 hours a year". [Psychophysics of frequency — book]
- E11. STAGE, DON'T ARGUE: no logical persuasion beats — use music, lighting, pacing, hands (unboxing, texture close-ups) to create the FEELING of the benefit; let the viewer post-rationalize. Test hook variants that change EMOTION (curiosity vs comfort), not arguments. ["The conscious mind thinks it's the Oval Office when it's really the press office" — book]

## 12. CUTS & SHOTS PATCHES — 2026-09-27 (editing-grammar deep research; practitioner consensus, not peer-reviewed)

Flat constant cadence is the "made by AI" tell. Every script now ships with a shot/cut plan, not just words.

- F1. CUT CADENCE: hard cut every 1.5–3s during demo/montage beats (5–15 cuts per 15s depending on format: hook 8–15, montage 7–15, talking-head 3–5, tutorial 2–4). No hold longer than 3–5s without a stated reason. Energy tiers: hot 0.8–1.5s / mid 2–3.5s / cool 4–6s — never 3 same-energy shots in a row. Vary pace deliberately.
- F2. SHOT-SCALE ROTATION: wide establishes space FIRST, then close-ups earn their moment — never 3–4 close-ups in a row with no establishing context (the #1 AI-pipeline failure). Rotation grammar: wide → medium → CU/ECU → reaction CU → wide. ECU reserved for hooks and reveals; CU for ~1s reaction shots after every action beat (action–reaction micro-loops); POV for tutorials/unboxing ("POV:" is a native hook format); top-down/flat-lay for demo clarity; macro for sensory/texture payoff.
- F3. CUT-ON-ACTION DEFAULT: cut mid-gesture, on the word boundary — the motion hides the edit. Jump cuts compress VO (cut every 1–3s, compress to 60–70% of raw). Match cuts are the native short-form transition (ease in/out ~80%, 1:2 pre/post timing). Smash cuts: max 1 per video, beat drops only.
- F4. NO CHEAP TRANSITIONS: hard cuts, seamless hand/object wipes (object must fully cover frame at the cut), whip pans (same direction/speed, motion blur hides the cut), zoom punch-ins, speed ramps (1.2–1.5x setup → 1.0x payoff). BANNED: dissolves, crossfades, wipes, fades, preset transition packs. If the render model can't land whip-pan motion blur, use a cut-on-action hard cut instead of a faked digital transition.
- F5. FIRST-3s SHOT GRAMMAR: frame 1 = face/product/text/motion — no logos, no dark static frames. Visible change by 0.5–2s. First cut lands by 1.5–2s. The hook gets ~50% of production effort and 3–5 A/B variants per body.
- F6. KARAOKE CAPTION SPEC (85%+ watch muted — captions ARE the delivery channel): 2–3 words per chunk (4 max); caption start = word onset + 0.08s, never early; entrance ≤250ms, exit ≈0; active word color-swap + 1.0→1.10→1.0 pop (~120ms, cap 1.15x); strip terminal punctuation; emoji ~1 per 8–10 words at phrase end; ≤3 flashes/sec; anchor to face, clear of platform UI. Captions carry pace on talking-head (word pops = visual change every ~0.3–0.5s); cuts carry on montages/demos (one idea per beat).
- F7. AI-RENDER FAILURE MODES (shot-list must defend against): clips generated in isolation don't cut together — shot list FIRST, shared visual-theme block in every prompt. Aimless slow push-ins read as "slideshow" — only endpoint-directed push-ins (push_to_ecu). Hands/faces: keep hands static or out of frame; never prompt chewing/eating hero shots ("sell the craving, not the bite"). Busy backgrounds are the subconscious uncanny tell — prefer plain. Never bake text/logos into generated clips — composite in post. Generate native 9:16.
- F8. PATTERN INTERRUPTS: every 2–5s, alternate the interrupt TYPE (scale change, angle change, speed ramp, caption pop, SFX sting) — repeating the same interrupt trains the eye to ignore it.
- F9. MACHINE-READABLE SHOT LIST: every script emits shot-list entries per ~/workspace/goals/ai-video-pipeline-build/hidden_files/shot-list-schema.json (shot_id, timeline, purpose, energy, shot_scale, angle, camera_move, subject, action [one verb+endpoint], setting, visual_theme_ref, continuity_mode, transitions, audio, caption, text_overlay, generation_prompt, model, aspect). The 10 validation rules in that file are enforced, not advisory.

## 13. HOOK DELIVERY PATCHES — 2026-09-27 (hook-delivery deep research; index-derived, not live-verified)

## §65 ATTENTION ENGINEERING DOCTRINE: HOOK + EVERY SHOT (2026-09-28, manual — PENDING LOOP VERIFICATION)

User order: "learn how you can engineer attention, in the hook and also in the
shots." Synthesized from 350 video-anatomy records in viral.db, today's three
reel learnings (emotion-gap hook formula, combo method, self-demonstrating
content), and external craft sources converged 2026-09-28. This section is the
unifying doctrine; it absorbs F8 (interrupt alternation), H10 (debt-carrying
interrupts), H13 (eye-path routing), H23 (re-hook cadence), and D2/D15
(event-specific re-hooks) into one operational model.

- THE COUNTDOWN MODEL: attention is a countdown that starts the instant the
reel appears. Every shot either resets the countdown or the viewer leaves.
~50–60% of drop-offs happen in the first 3 seconds; viewers who survive 3s
mostly survive 10s+. Design job: make leaving the harder option, per shot.
- TWO LAYERS, DIFFERENT JOBS, DIFFERENT CADENCES. They are complementary, not
alternatives — a video with only one layer leaks viewers:
  - VISUAL LAYER (keep the EYE busy): an interrupt every 2–5s — angle change,
scale change, caption pop, SFX sting, speed ramp, cut on music beat. MUST
alternate type (F8): repeating one interrupt trains the eye to ignore it.
~85% watch muted, so on-screen text doubles as the interrupt — subtitles
alone add ~+12% retention.
  - STORY LAYER (keep the MIND pulling): a re-hook every ~7–10s — open a new
loop ("but there's a catch I didn't see coming"), tease a payoff ("the
third one is the one that moved the needle"), bump the tension, or hard
turn ("forget everything I just said, because…"). Our own data: across 350
anatomies, mean beat cadence is ~1 attention event / 8s — the story layer
band. External craft converges on ~9s re-hook cadence.
- THE HOOK IS INTERRUPT + OPEN LOOP, FUSED. Two loads, one frame:
  1) ARREST (stop the thumb): an emotional jolt — norm violation ("you
shouldn't be doing that"), absurdity, skill freeze, impossible image.
  2) DEBT (convert the stop into watch time): a curiosity gap only the video
can pay ("what the hell is she doing?"). Curiosity alone is the weaker half
— the adley/combo-method reel proved emotion does the heavy lifting.
Pattern interrupt inside the first 5s → ~23% higher average retention.
- SHOT-LEVEL LAW (new): no shot survives past ~5s without a visual change.
Key content lands at SCENE STARTS — attention peaks ~0.66s after a cut, so
the first beat of every scene carries the payload, never the tail.
- DEBT TEST (extends H10): every interrupt must carry curiosity debt that only
the product/story can pay. Deletion test: cut the interrupt — if the viewer
misses nothing, it was decoration, and decoration is noise.
- ONE LIVE THREAD MINIMUM: keep at least one unresolved open loop alive at all
times. Open the next loop BEFORE closing the current one — the handoff, not
the closure, is what holds the viewer. The final button closes the opening
loop (≤8 words) so the reel loops cleanly.
